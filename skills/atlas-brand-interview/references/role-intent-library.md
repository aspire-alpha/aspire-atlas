# Role-shaped intent libraries

Turn 2 of the brand interview. **Pick the library that matches the role captured
in Turn 1.** Frame each option as what is *not working* — people name a pain more
accurately than they name a job.

Four to six options, plus an own-words escape. Adapt the wording to what the brand
profile surfaced; a pain that names their real entities beats a generic one.

---

## Influencer / creator marketing
- finding creators who actually fit takes forever
- can't tell which creators are safe to sign
- no idea who's already talking about us
- proving creator ROI to leadership is painful

## Performance / paid
- creative fatigue — running out of winning content
- don't know which organic posts deserve spend
- competitors' creators and content are a black box
- no benchmarks to judge our numbers against

## Social & content
- never sure what to post next
- engagement is flat and I don't know why
- can't keep up with trends
- mentions of us go unseen

## Brand / comms
- afraid of partner content blowing up on us
- no view of sentiment around the brand
- competitor moves catch us off guard

## Agency (working for a client)
- client reporting eats hours every week
- monitoring several brands at once
- proving value at renewal time

## Founder / CMO / generalist
- no visibility into whether any of this is working
- no time to watch social myself
- nothing forwardable to investors or buyers

---

## The mismatches that break trust

The point of splitting identity from intent is to avoid these. Each one tells the
user Atlas guessed instead of asking.

| Offered to | Why it lands wrong |
|---|---|
| A **CMO**, "finding creators worth signing" | Sourcing is two levels below them. They want to know if the programme works and whether it's forwardable. |
| A **junior influencer marketer**, "nothing forwardable to investors" | Not their audience, not their remit. |
| An **agency**, anything phrased around a single brand | They run several, and renewal is the pressure. |
| A **performance marketer**, creator-sourcing tasks | They buy media; creative supply and spend allocation are the pains. |
| A **social manager**, "proving ROI to leadership" | They post. Attribution is someone else's fight. |

## Seniority also changes what they can answer

Carry this forward — Step 4 depends on it. A founder or CMO can credibly speak for
company strategy; an IC cannot. When the role is an IC, reframe strategic
questions to their own scope, or take the researched read and mark it
`(inferred, unconfirmed)`. Never make someone invent an answer on their company's
behalf.
