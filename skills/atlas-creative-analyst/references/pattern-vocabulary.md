# What counts as a pattern

A pattern is a **feature of the content** that a creator could reproduce on
purpose. If reproducing it requires knowing the performance data, it is not a
pattern — it is a description.

| Not a pattern | Why | The pattern underneath, if any |
|---|---|---|
| "Your top posts get more comments" | Circular — that's the metric | — |
| "Retail news performs well" | An event, not a reproducible feature | Posts that share news the audience feels part of |
| "Authentic content works" | Unactionable | Unscripted single-take delivery, no captions burnt in |
| "Video outperforms images" | Format-level, usually a platform artefact | Whatever the video is doing that the image isn't |
| "@creator-x does well for you" | A creator, not a pattern | The thing that creator does that others don't |

## Feature families worth testing

**Opening** — first two seconds. Face, product, text, motion, question.
**Person** — present or absent, on camera or hands-only, one or several.
**Product integration** — in use, held, worn, spoken about, absent.
**Claim type** — demonstration, comparison, before/after, testimonial, none.
**Setting** — kitchen, aisle, studio, outdoors, in-car.
**Text on screen** — from `analysis.overlayText`. Presence, density, whether it
carries the hook or repeats the caption.
**Caption shape** — question to the audience, announcement, gratitude, product
claim, story.
**Pacing and length** — cut frequency, duration band.
**Tone** — humorous, sincere, instructional, deadpan.

Cross two families before three. A two-family pattern ("person + question
caption") is briefable; a four-family one is almost certainly overfitted to the
handful of posts that produced it.

## The Yough worked example — why the obvious read was wrong

The alignment snapshot said: *retail news and community prompts out-engage product
shots roughly 3×.*

The Creative Analyst has to ask what is reproducible in that. Looking at the actual
top three:

| Post | Engagement | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| "This says a LOT about you…" | 1,915 likes / 676 comments | An **opinion prompt** — no retail, no product |
| Sprouts nationwide announcement | 1,215 / 166 | Retail news |
| Momentum / gratitude post | 640 / 58 | Neither |

Two candidate patterns fit: **announcements**, or **posts that ask the audience
something.** Those are different briefs — one needs news to exist, the other can be
made any week. The top performer supports the second, not the first.

**With 101 posts and likes withheld on 45, neither clears the bar.** The correct
output is the uncertain bucket plus a test: run three audience-question posts in
weeks with no news, and compare comment volume against the 30-day median.

This is the shape of the job. The obvious read — "do more retail news" — is
unbriefable *and* probably wrong, and finding that out is the value.

## Phrases that signal overclaiming

Catch these in your own draft:

- "because" or "drives" where you mean "is associated with"
- any causal claim from a single month
- a ratio with no n beside it
- "consistently" on fewer than eight posts
- a pattern with no counterexample offered — it means you didn't look
- "clearly" or "obviously" — if it were, the brand would already know
