# Alignment target library — role-shaped candidates

Atlas users are marketing-team people. Draft Q1 and Q2 candidates from the role
captured in Step 3, then present 3–4 as a select with an own-words escape.

**These are candidates, not a menu to recite.** Adapt the wording to what the
brand profile actually surfaced. A candidate that names their real entities
(*"hold the Target placement"*) beats a generic one every time.

## 90-day candidates by role

**Primary question for ICs and agencies.** For leadership, do not ask these cold
— derive the 90-day from the two-year they just gave you: *"what has to be true
this quarter for that to be on track?"* Then confirm in one line.

**Influencer / creator marketer**
- Cut time from shortlist to signed creator, with no safety surprises
- Stand up a partner roster of {N} who fit, not just who reply
- Know who is already posting about us unpaid, and convert the best of them
- Produce something on creator performance my leadership actually reads

**Performance / paid**
- Find organic creator posts worth putting spend behind, weekly
- Break creative fatigue — a refreshed pool of proven content angles
- See which creators and content the competitor set is running
- Get benchmarks to judge our numbers against

**Social & content**
- Always know what to post next, grounded in what is working in-category
- Understand why engagement is flat and which themes are moving
- Stop missing mentions of us
- Find the creator content worth reposting

**Brand / comms**
- Never be surprised by partner content again — a standard that actually screens
- See sentiment around the brand and its partners
- Catch competitor moves before they land as news

**Agency (for a client)**
- Cut client reporting from hours to minutes
- Monitor several brands without switching context
- Have defensible evidence at renewal

**Founder / CMO / generalist**
- Know whether creator marketing is working at all
- Get something forwardable to investors or a retail buyer
- Stop needing to watch social myself

## Two-year candidates

**Only asked in depth of leadership** — CMO, founder, head of marketing. For a
manager, scope it to their function. For an IC, do not ask it as a company
question at all: take the researched read, mark it `(inherited)`, and move on.

For leadership this is the *primary* question, so give it the depth the 90-day
gets for an IC — draft candidates, let them correct one, then derive the 90-day
from it.

- Be the brand our category thinks of first
- Own a content territory competitors cannot buy their way into
- Run a creator program that compounds — partners who stay and grow with us
- Make creator content the primary acquisition channel, not a supporting one
- Hold and expand the retail placement the year turns on
- Build a partner bench deep enough that no single creator is load-bearing

## Cadence defaults

Let their answer set this. Where they have no view, these are the defaults the
customer corpus supports:

| Kind of target | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Risk, compliance, disclosure | **interrupt** | Late is the same as never |
| Mentions, spikes | interrupt or daily | Depends on volume |
| Roster activity, go-lives | daily | Cheap and universal |
| Competitor moves, themes, SOV | weekly | Needs time to mean anything |
| Discovery, shortlists | weekly or on demand | Rarely urgent |
| Reporting rollups | their reporting day | Ask which day |

Rule of thumb from the corpus: **compliance and risk interrupt; discovery and
trends digest.** Do not make them configure this — infer it from how they
described their week, and confirm in one line.

**Cadence feeds the four passive channels Step 5 arms** — social listening, brand
safety, competitive analysis, industry benchmarking — and the recurring brief that
`atlas-brief` produces. It is metadata for those channels, not a brief
subscription the user has to design at minute 10.

## Restating goals — patterns that hold up

| They say | Restate as | Not covered |
|---|---|---|
| "Grow revenue from creators" | Creator activity and reach relative to each creator's baseline; overlap converted to signed partners | Revenue attribution — the store isn't connected |
| "Improve ROI / lower CAC" | Which content and creators over-perform their own baseline | Cost and conversion data |
| "More brand awareness" | Untagged category mentions; theme ownership vs the competitor set; SOV once the watchlist accrues | Surveys, search volume, recall |
| "Better quality creators" | Fit against the ideal-creator spec; safety verdicts; competing-brand saturation | Audience authenticity — **no signal exists** |
| "Avoid a PR problem" | Screening against the applied standard; disclosure presence in caption and overlay text | Drift since approval — no verdict history |
| "Grow to N followers" | Nothing. Push back gently | Follower goals were requested in **zero of 155** customer asks |
| "Beat {competitor}" | Creator overlap, theme comparison, shared hashtag territory | Their sales, their spend, their roadmap |

**When the honest answer is "Atlas can't measure that outcome":** say it, then
name the leading indicator it *can* watch, and be explicit about who owns which
half. *"You'll measure the revenue. I'll tell you whether the creator side is
moving, and which creators moved it."* That division of labor is more credible
than a dashboard that implies attribution Atlas does not have.
