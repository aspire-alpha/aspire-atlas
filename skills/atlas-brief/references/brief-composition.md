# What goes in a brief

Three to five items. Each one must pass all four tests, or it doesn't ship.

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **New** | Did this change since the last edition? Not "is it true" — *did it move.* |
| **Targeted** | Does it serve a target in `alignment-targets.md`? If not, cut it. |
| **Evidenced** | Is there a post, a handle, a number behind it, linked? |
| **Decidable** | Does it end in a decision or a question they can answer? |

A fact that passes the first three and fails the fourth is trivia. Put it in the
page's history section or drop it.

## Item shapes that work

Drawn from the four passive channels.

**Something moved on the gap**
> Your gap narrowed. @mornings.with.dez posted the routine angle on Tuesday and it
> saved 4,100 — your best creator post to date, 43× her usual. → *Brief two more
> like it?*

**A competitor made a move**
> Halo & Hearth signed three creators this week. Two of them have posted about you
> unpaid in the last month. → *Want the list before they're locked in?*

**Someone new is talking about you**
> Four new creators mentioned you since Monday, all unprompted, all in the routine
> theme. One is at 2.8× her normal engagement. → *Worth reaching out?*

**A risk surfaced**
> @theshelfedit posted something that trips your standard on unverified health
> claims. It's live now. → *Want me to show you before you decide?*

**A benchmark shifted**
> Your saves-per-post is up 34% over four weeks while the category is flat. The
> change tracks with person-on-camera posts. → *Push more of it?*

Note the shape: **what changed, the evidence, then a question.** Not a metric with
a delta arrow.

## What never goes in

- **Restated metrics.** "You have 61,400 followers" is not news.
- **"No change" rows.** If nothing changed on a channel, say nothing about it.
- **Anything from the last edition**, unless it materially moved — and then lead
  with the movement, not the item.
- **Things that serve no target.** Interesting is not the bar; relevant is.
- **Unanalyzed content presented as clean.** If a post hasn't been looked at
  properly, say so on the item or leave it out.
- **Predictions.** Report what happened. A forecast with no evidence behind it is
  the fastest way to be wrong on the record.
- **Anything you'd have to overclaim to say.** Borrow the creative analyst's bar:
  if the data can't carry it, the honest line is that it can't.

## The quiet week is a designed state, not a failure

Most weeks, for most brands, little of consequence happens. A brief that admits
this keeps its credibility; one that fills the space loses it in a month.

> Quiet week. The gap is where it was, no new mentions, roster steady. Back Monday.

Two lines. No sections, no "no change" table, no apology. **Publish it anyway** —
the page updating with an honest quiet note is what proves the thing is running.

If three consecutive editions are quiet, say something more useful once:

> Third quiet week. Nothing's moving on its own — if you want something to move,
> the fastest lever is still the routine brief we talked about.

That is a nudge, not filler, because it points at a decision.

## Ordering

**Most consequential first, not most recent.** A risk that's live now outranks a
competitor move from Tuesday. Recency is a tiebreak, never the sort.

If one item clearly dominates, lead with it alone and let the rest sit under it —
same shape as the snapshot's headline gap.

## Length

The whole brief should be readable in **under a minute**. If it isn't, cut the
weakest item rather than trimming all of them — four strong items beat five where
one is thin, and a thin item makes the others look padded.
